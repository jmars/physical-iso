## Direct answer

**CNS varies parameters inside a fixed law-structure, and Smolin has never proposed selection over a
logic/grammar-type rule-set or over the conservation laws themselves.**

1. The 1992 origin paper states the mutation channel explicitly: at a bounce "the dimensionless
   parameters of the standard models of particle physics and cosmology undergo small random changes",
   and selection favours "those choices of parameters that lead to universes that produce the most
   black holes during their lifetime" **[FETCHED: INSPIRE, Smo92 abstract, verbatim]**. The objects
   of variation are dimensionless constants and couplings. Not one law-statement is in the mutating
   set.
2. In the 2006 status paper the *scope* is stated as a virtue: "the master prediction M refers to the
   observed low energy parameters and not to the fundamental landscape of theories", and earlier "these
   conditions all refer to the space of parameters of the standard model" **[FETCHED: arXiv PDF,
   hep-th/0612185, §4 and §2]**. CNS is deliberately a theory *of the parameter point*, held aloof
   even from the space of fundamental theories.
3. The quantum-relativistic framework is a *hypothesis*, not an output. That is how the critical
   literature reads it too: CNS "explains why the parameters of physics possess the values we observe
   them to possess, but depends upon the contingent fact that the universe is a quantum relativistic
   universe" **[FETCHED: arXiv/INSPIRE, McC06 abstract, verbatim]**.
4. Where Smolin *does* later say laws evolve, he means dynamical law, and the conservation laws stay
   as constraints. In the precedence proposal, a novel system's possible outcomes "are still
   constrained by symmetries. They are also constrained by the conservation laws that follow from
   symmetries including energy" **[FETCHED: arXiv PDF, 1205.3707, §2, verbatim]**. What evolves there
   is the *selection of outcomes* from precedent, i.e. the probabilistic dynamics — not which
   processes exist.
5. In *Temporal naturalism* the slogan is "laws of nature evolve in that time" **[FETCHED: INSPIRE,
   1310.8539 abstract]**, and the illustrative metalaw is still parameter-level: a "statistical form
   of metalaw, as is posited in cosmological natural selection, by the stipulation that changes in the
   parameters of the standard model resulting from bounces are small and random"
   **[FETCHED: arXiv PDF, 1310.8539 §8.2]**. The one place the aspiration is stated in full is a
   **Peirce quotation** Smolin endorses: "the only possible way of accounting for the laws of nature
   and for uniformity in general is to suppose them results of evolution" **[FETCHED: arXiv PDF,
   hep-th/0612185 §4, quoting Peirce]** — an aspiration, with no mechanism attached that acts on a
   rule-set.
6. On the specific pair this paper cares about, Smolin's record is the opposite of "conservation is
   negotiable". He once conjectured that "if singularities are not eliminated by quantum effects, loss
   of information will result" **[FETCHED: INSPIRE, gr-qc/9301016 abstract]** — a conditional
   *dynamics* consequence in quantum cosmology — and by 2010 he classifies information loss among the
   "radical options" and argues "that restoring unitary evolution relies on elimination of
   singularities" **[FETCHED: INSPIRE, 0901.3156 abstract]**. So unitarity is a constraint to be
   *achieved*, and information loss is a defect of a dynamics. Nowhere is "no conservation" a lawful
   sector, and nowhere is no-cloning or unitarity a selected variable.

**Net:** Smolin is the right citation for *selection-on-stability as explanatory logic*, and the
wrong citation for *selection over conservation*. The nearest he comes to a rule-level selection is
an aspiration (Peirce) and a variational principle (maximal variety, below), neither of which
considers a world whose conservation theorems fail. His universe ensemble is also a **breeder** —
reproduction, heredity, differential fitness in black-hole count — which is precisely what this
paper's §5.5 signature ("lossy, not a breeder") says the pre-lawful sector is not.

## Closest precedents

### A. The CNS baseline: selection over parameters

**Smo92 — L. Smolin, "Did the Universe Evolve?", Classical and Quantum Gravity 9 (1992) 173–192,
DOI 10.1088/0264-9381/9/1/016** **[FETCHED: INSPIRE]**. Claims: bounces re-draw dimensionless
parameters at random; the ensemble converges toward a local maximum of black-hole production;
therefore small changes to our parameters should lower it. *Proximity:* high on the **logic**
(selection by a stability/fitness criterion, no observer), zero on the **object** (parameters, with
the standard-model form and QM/GR untouched).

**Smo97 — L. Smolin, *The Life of the Cosmos*, Oxford University Press, 1997, ISBN 978-0-19-512664-8
(UK: Weidenfeld & Nicolson)** **[FETCHED: Open Library; the same two publishers are listed in
Smo06's reference [2], FETCHED: arXiv PDF]**. The book-length statement of the program. *Proximity:*
the popular-level home of the parameter-selection logic. **[MEMORY]** the book also speculates about
deeper law-evolution; I did not read the book this session and assert nothing about its text beyond
being the program's book statement — see human-check item H1.

**Smo06 — L. Smolin, "The status of cosmological natural selection", arXiv:hep-th/0612185 (Dec
2006)** **[FETCHED: arXiv/INSPIRE]**; no journal reference in INSPIRE, so venue is needs-human.
Claims: falsifiability conditions for a landscape theory; CNS meets them; CNS survives Vilenkin's
objection; the master prediction is about low-energy parameters, explicitly not the fundamental
landscape; Peirce quoted for the laws-evolve ambition. *Proximity:* the single most useful citation
for this paper, because it contains the **scope disclaimer in Smolin's own words** — the sentence a
referee would otherwise suspect us of inventing.

**Smo04 — L. Smolin, "Scientific alternatives to the anthropic principle", in B. Carr (ed.),
*Universe or Multiverse?*, Cambridge University Press (2007), pp. 323–366; arXiv:hep-th/0407213**
**[FETCHED: INSPIRE]** (INSPIRE links it to the book record "Universe or multiverse? — Cambridge, UK:
Univ. Pr. (2007) 517 p"). Claims: the anthropic principle is not falsifiable; selection principles
inside one universe are ordinary science; CNS is the falsifiable multiverse example. *Proximity:*
this is the paper that makes "selection, not anthropic" a defensible move — useful precisely because
our §5.5 must not be read as anthropic.

**Vaa02 — R. Vaas, "Is there a Darwinian evolution of the cosmos? Some comments on Lee Smolin's
theory of the origin of universes by means of natural selection", arXiv:gr-qc/0205119 (2002)**
**[FETCHED: arXiv/INSPIRE]**. Claims (independent description plus critique): in CNS "the values of
the free parameters of the physical laws are reprocessed and slightly changed"; the Darwinian analogy
is weak (fitness is internal — black-hole count — and universes do not compete), and falsifiability
is doubtful. *Proximity:* the best third-party confirmation of the parameter-only reading, and a
ready-made list of the objections a referee will raise against *any* "cosmic selection" claim,
including ours if we lean too hard on the word "selection".

### B. The law-level extensions, ranked by proximity to selection over rules

**McC06 — G. McCabe, "Extending cosmological natural selection", arXiv:gr-qc/0610016 (2006)**
**[FETCHED: arXiv/INSPIRE]**. Claims: Smolin's hypothesis explains parameters but assumes a
quantum-relativistic universe; that assumption can itself be explained "by the notion of evolution
towards stable ('rigid') mathematical structures" **[verbatim]**. *Proximity:* **the closest thing in
this literature to our move** — the selected object is promoted from constants to *structure*, and
the criterion is stability. But the structures are mathematical-physical frameworks, not a formal
grammar; no rule is varied one at a time, no theorem is derived about what fails outside the stable
set, and conservation laws are not among the varied items.

**Alt13 — L. Altenberg, "Implications of the Reduction Principle for Cosmological Natural
Selection", arXiv:1302.1293 (2013)** **[FETCHED: arXiv/INSPIRE]**. Claims: "the laws for variation and
inheritance of the parameters are also subject to CNS if variation in transmission laws occurs"
**[verbatim]**; Feldman's Reduction Principle then favours faithful replication, so selection has two
objects — fitness and inheritance fidelity. *Proximity:* a genuine **meta-selection** (selection
acting on rules *about* variation), the nearest formal cousin inside the CNS program. But the
meta-level is transmission/inheritance of parameters — a genetics of universes — not the logical
rule-set, and it too assumes the physics whose laws we vary.

**Smo15T — L. Smolin, "Temporal naturalism", Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics 52
(2015) 86–102, DOI 10.1016/j.shpsb.2015.03.005** **[FETCHED: INSPIRE]** (full text
arXiv:1310.8539 **[FETCHED: arXiv PDF]**). Claims: time is real and "laws of nature evolve in that
time"; the "metalaw dilemma" (regress vs unreason) is dissolved by statistical metalaws, CNS named as
one; earlier physicists (Dirac, Feynman) quoted for law-evolution. *Proximity:* the program's
strongest **philosophical** claim that laws are mutable — the claim our §5.5 could be mistaken for.
The mechanisms it then offers are parameter mutation and precedent; there is no rule-set, no
discard/no-discard axis, and no theorem.

**Smo15P — L. Smolin, "Precedence and freedom in quantum physics", International Journal of Quantum
Foundations 1(1) (2015) 44–55; arXiv:1205.3707** **[FETCHED: INSPIRE; full text arXiv PDF]**. Claims:
outcomes are drawn from the ensemble of precedents; a novel system's outcomes are undetermined but
still "constrained by the conservation laws that follow from symmetries including energy"; therefore
dynamical laws can evolve as new precedents accumulate. *Proximity:* the sharpest **counter-evidence**
to a loose reading of "Smolin says laws evolve" — in his most law-evolutionary proposal, conservation
is a fixed constraint and no-cloning/unitarity are untouched.

**Smo16V — L. Smolin, "Quantum mechanics and the principle of maximal variety", Foundations of
Physics 46 (2016) 736–758, DOI 10.1007/s10701-016-9994-x; arXiv:1506.02938** **[FETCHED:
INSPIRE abstract]**. Claims: "Quantum mechanics is derived from the principle that the universe
contain as much variety as possible" **[verbatim]**; the extremal principle yields the Schrödinger
dynamics and dissolves the measurement problem. *Proximity:* a **variational selection over the form
of a physical theory** — the closest Smolin gets to "the laws are picked by a criterion". But the
criterion selects among dynamics *within* quantum theory, deriving QM rather than selecting a
rule-set, and the derived structure is QM's own (hence its conservation laws).

**Pei91 — C. S. Peirce, "The architecture of theories", The Monist (1891); reprinted in J. Buchler
(ed.), *Philosophical Writings of Peirce*, Dover (1955)** **[FETCHED: the citation is transcribed from
Smo06's reference [44], arXiv PDF; the wording below is Smolin's quotation of Peirce, same source]**.
Claims: "Law is par excellence the thing that wants a reason … the only possible way of accounting
for the laws of nature and for uniformity in general is to suppose them results of evolution"
**[verbatim as quoted]**. *Proximity:* the **ancestral statement of the ambition** our paper
partially realises — and the honest way to cite it is as an ambition Smolin inherits and CNS only
partly executes.

## Novelty verdict

**PARTIALLY-NOVEL.**

*Preceded (do not claim these):*

- the **explanatory logic** — select a sector by stability/lawfulness rather than postulate it, with
  no observer and no fine-tuning: Smo92, Smo97, Smo04, Smo06. Our §5.5 already labels this as
  ancestry and is right to.
- **selection over structures rather than constants**: McC06 (explicitly "stable ('rigid')
  mathematical structures").
- **meta-selection over the rules of variation**: Alt13 (Reduction Principle over inheritance laws).
- **the laws-evolve aspiration**: Pei91 as quoted and endorsed by Smolin; Smo15T; and a variational
  selection over the form of a theory in Smo16V.

*Novel — no fetched source does any of these:*

- **the object**: a formal rule-set in which the selected variable is a *structural permission* — the
  discard ban — rather than a number, a structure, or an inheritance law. Outside the kernel
  information loss is a **theorem about derivations** (`Dcare.no_vanishing_unrestricted_refuted`), not
  a parameter value; inside it, conservation is a theorem
  (`Dcare.no_vanishing_restricted`). Nothing in the Smolin lineage selects over a grammar, and nothing
  in it has a theorem saying what the unchosen sector *is*.
- **the criterion is theorem-shaped, not fitness-shaped**: stability is proved (divergence:
  `adversary_spin_diverges`, `spin_pays_forever_buys_nothing`), so the "selection" is a consistency
  filter evaluated once, with no population, no heredity, no mutation amplitude, no reproduction
  channel. CNS is a Darwinian mechanism and needs all four; this is a **filter on rule-sets**, and the
  difference should be stated, because a referee who has read Smolin will otherwise ask where the
  heredity is.
- **the signature**: "lossy, not a breeder" — cloning stays forbidden in the unrestricted calculus
  (`no_contraction_content` holds at full grain) while vanishing is permitted. That asymmetry is a
  property of the calculus and has no counterpart in CNS, whose ensemble breeds by construction.
- **the units**: no cosmological ensemble, no bounce, no black-hole fitness, no parameter space —
  hence no dependence on quantum gravity, inflation or landscape assumptions that CNS is criticised
  for (Vaa02).

*Consequence for §5.5 as written:* the section's current sentence puts the lineage's descent through
"[Whe57]" and "the anthropic literature [Car74]". Smolin's CNS is explicitly **anti**-anthropic
(Smo04), so citing Car74 and Smolin in one breath would be a category error a referee will catch. The
defensible form is: Wheeler's foam as ancestry, Carter as the anthropic variant this reading does not
need, and **CNS as the falsifiable, parameter-level member of the same selection-on-stability family,
cited for the logic and distinguished on the object**.

## What a human should still check

- **H1 — the 1997 book's text.** I verified its metadata (Open Library) but did not read it. The
  parameter-only reading of the *book* rests on Smo92's abstract, Smo06's scope sentence and Vaas's
  description, not on the book itself. If §5.5 cites Smo97, someone should confirm the book never
  claims that the conservation laws themselves vary — Smo15T's "laws evolve" is the place the
  opposite impression could come from, and it postdates the book.
- **H2 — page range of Smo92.** INSPIRE says 173–**192**; Smolin's own reference list in Smo06 says
  "9, 173–191" **[FETCHED, both]**. One of them is a typo; pick the journal's own record.
- **H3 — Smo06's venue.** INSPIRE lists no journal or book for hep-th/0612185. If it appeared in a
  collection (it reads like a review for one), the citation should name it; otherwise cite the arXiv
  identifier and say preprint.
- **H4 — Peirce 1891 details.** The Monist volume/pages are unverified; I have only Smolin's own
  reference [44]. Verify or drop the row and cite Peirce only inside the Smolin citation's prose.
- **H5 — Found. Phys. 43, 21 (Smo13L).** INSPIRE gives an article number (artid 21) rather than a
  page range; confirm the journal's preferred form.
- **H6 — the anti-anthropic clause must be in the text.** §5.5 currently reads the lineage as
  descending "from … the anthropic literature [Car74]". If Smolin is added there, the sentence needs
  its distinguishing clause, because Smo04's whole argument is that anthropic reasoning is not
  science. Suggested insertion (one sentence, to be edited into §5.5 by whoever owns the paper, not
  by this scout):

  > The falsifiable, parameter-level member of that family is cosmological natural selection, in which
  > bounces re-draw the dimensionless constants and black-hole production does the selecting
  > [Smo92, Smo06]; it is explicitly not anthropic [Smo04], and what it varies is the parameter
  > point, not the laws — Smolin's own scope statement is that the prediction concerns "the observed
  > low energy parameters and not to the fundamental landscape of theories" [Smo06]. What this paper
  > selects over is the rule-set itself: the discard ban, whose absence is a theorem about
  > derivations rather than a value of a constant.

- **H7 — the novelty sentence must be phrased against the *object*, not the *logic*.** The logic is
  Smolin's and McCabe's; claiming novelty for "selection on stability" would be false and is exactly
  the kind of overclaim the paper's label discipline exists to prevent. Novelty lives in: rule-set as
  the selected object, theorem-shaped criterion, no reproduction, and the lossy-not-a-breeder
  asymmetry.
- **H8 — a possible referee objection to pre-empt.** CNS is a *Darwinian* mechanism (variation,
  heredity, differential reproduction). Our reading has none of the three and calls itself selection
  anyway. Either add half a sentence saying "selection in the filter sense — consistency, not
  reproduction", or expect the objection. The paper's §5.5 currently says "the selection is
  theorem-shaped", which is the right instinct but does not yet name the absence of heredity.
